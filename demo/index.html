Plants turn light into food.

Photosynthesis lets plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to make sugar for energy, releasing oxygen as a by-product.

Photosynthesis happens in chloroplasts. Chlorophyll absorbs sunlight and uses its energy to turn water and carbon dioxide into glucose, which feeds the plant. Oxygen is released through tiny pores called stomata. Almost all life on Earth depends on this process.

Photosynthesis happens in two linked stages inside chloroplasts. In the light-dependent stage, chlorophyll absorbs sunlight and splits water, releasing oxygen and storing energy in ATP and NADPH. In the Calvin cycle that follows, that stored energy is used to fix carbon dioxide into glucose. The plant uses the sugar to grow, or stores it as starch. Almost every food chain begins here, and it is also why plants are vital to the oxygen in our air.
